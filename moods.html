Moods & emotions

Naming a feeling precisely is a skill. The better you can name it, the easier it is to handle.

Emotion

A quick reaction to something that just happened. Lasts seconds to minutes. "I jumped when the door slammed."

Mood

A background colour that lasts hours or days, often with no clear cause. "I've been grumpy all day."

Temperament

Your usual long-term tendency. "I'm generally a calm person."

The feelings wheel

Most people stop at "good", "bad", "mad" or "sad". Tap a core feeling, then tap a more exact word to learn what it means, what it feels like in the body, and what it usually needs.

Mood check-in: rate yourself on two scales: energy (low ↔ high) and pleasantness (unpleasant ↔ pleasant). Together they give four "mood zones".

Voice tone

The same words can mean opposite things depending on how they're said.

🎵 Pitch

High or low. Rising pitch sounds like a question or excitement; falling pitch sounds final, sure or bored.

⏱️ Pace

Fast can mean excited, nervous or annoyed. Slow can mean calm, sad or "I'm explaining this very clearly".

🔊 Volume

Loud means energy or anger. Quiet can mean shy, sad, secretive or dangerously calm.

Passive-aggressiveness

Showing anger or annoyance indirectly, so it can always be denied: "What? I said it was fine."

Common signs

  • "Fine." / "Whatever." Agreeing in words but not in tone.
  • Silent treatment: withdrawing to punish instead of talking.
  • Backhanded compliments: "You look great, for once!"
  • Sarcasm as a shield: "Oh no, take your time, I love waiting."
  • Deliberate "forgetting" or doing a task badly on purpose.
  • Pointed reminders: "As I already said…", "Per my last message…"
  • Hinting to others instead of talking to the person: vague posts, "some people…"
  • Fake cheerfulness that doesn't match the situation: "No worries!!! 🙂"

Why people do it

  • They're afraid of conflict or of being seen as "mean".
  • They grew up where showing anger openly wasn't safe or allowed.
  • They want the other person to guess what's wrong.
  • They feel powerless to change things directly.

How to respond

  1. Stay calm and don't match the tone.
  2. Name it gently: "You said it's fine, but you sound annoyed. Is something up?"
  3. Ask about the real issue and listen.
  4. Set a limit if it keeps happening: "I'd rather you tell me straight when you're upset."

Body language & faces

The body adds clues, but one signal alone means nothing. Look for groups of signals, changes from normal, and context.

Myth: "Crossed arms always means defensive." It can also mean cold, comfy or just a habit. Myth: "Liars won't look you in the eye." Plenty of honest people avoid eye contact, including many autistic people and people from cultures where steady eye contact is rude.

"Dere" character types

Anime and manga describe love interests by how they show affection. "Dere" comes from deredere, Japanese for being lovey-dovey.

Tap a card to flip it. Each card has a real-life lens: what this behaviour would mean if a real person did it.

Yandere in real life = not romantic. In fiction, a yandere's obsession is played for drama or thrills. In real life, jealousy that controls you, checking your phone, cutting you off from friends, or threats ("if you leave, I'll hurt myself / you") are warning signs of an abusive relationship, not proof of love. If something feels wrong

Talk to someone you trust: a friend, parent, teacher or school counsellor. In the US you can text LOVEIS to 22522 or visit loveisrespect.org (for relationships). If you're in crisis, call or text 988. Outside the US, search for your local helpline. If someone is in danger right now, call emergency services.
